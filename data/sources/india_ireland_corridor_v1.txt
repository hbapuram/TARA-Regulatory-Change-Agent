FEMA Master Direction on Deposits & Accounts (NRI provisions) and the
Agreement between the Government of Ireland and the Government of the
Republic of India for the Avoidance of Double Taxation — combined corridor
guidance for a holder who is both an Indian citizen and an Irish tax
resident

Instrument: RBI Master Direction on Deposits & Accounts (NRI provisions) +
            India-Ireland Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA)
Issuing authority: Reserve Bank of India / Central Board of Direct Taxes
Provision: Section 6.2 — FEMA reclassification of resident accounts

Section 6.1 — Application
This corridor guidance applies only to a holder who is, at the same time, an
Indian citizen and tax resident of Ireland. Neither fact alone brings a
holder within this section: an Indian citizen who is also Indian tax
resident is covered by domestic Indian guidance alone, and an Irish tax
resident who is not an Indian citizen has no FEMA non-resident-account
status to consider. It is the combination — citizenship of one jurisdiction,
tax residency of the other — that gives rise to the obligations below, none
of which appears in either jurisdiction's own single-country guidance.

Section 6.2 — FEMA reclassification of resident accounts
Under the Foreign Exchange Management Act, an Indian citizen who becomes
resident outside India for tax purposes must not continue to hold a
resident (domestic) savings account in India. Any such account must be
redesignated as a Non-Resident Ordinary (NRO) account, or a Non-Resident
External (NRE) account opened in its place, within a reasonable period of
the change in residency status. Continuing to operate a resident account
after ceasing to be an Indian tax resident is a contravention of FEMA
regulations administered by the Reserve Bank of India.

Section 6.3 — DTAA relief and Form 10F / Tax Residency Certificate
Where India-source income (such as interest on an NRO account, or dividends
from Indian equity) arises to a holder who is tax resident of Ireland under
the India-Ireland Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement, relief from double
taxation on that income — either by way of exemption or a reduced rate of
withholding — may be claimed under the Agreement. To claim it, the holder
must obtain a Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) from the Irish Revenue
Commissioners for the relevant year and furnish it, together with a
completed Form 10F, to the Indian payer or tax authority before the
relief is applied.
