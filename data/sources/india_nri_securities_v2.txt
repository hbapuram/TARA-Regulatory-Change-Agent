CAPITAL GAINS TAX — TRANSFER OF LISTED SECURITIES BY A NON-RESIDENT INDIAN
Central Board of Direct Taxes
Consolidated extract for TARA domain pack `india-nri-securities`

Note on recodification: the Income-tax Act, 2025 took effect 1 April 2026
and renumbers most of the Income-tax Act, 1961. The provisions below are
stated by their substance and their original 1961-Act section numbers
(Sections 111A, 112A, 115, 139, 195), which are the numbers still used in
practitioner guidance as of this drafting. Confirm the corresponding
2025-Act section numbers against the CBDT's published concordance table
before quoting them to a filer.

Section 1.1 — Character of the gain
Gain on the transfer of an equity share in a company, being a security
listed on a recognised stock exchange in India in respect of which
securities transaction tax has been paid, is a long-term capital gain where
the share was held for more than twelve months immediately preceding the
date of transfer, and a short-term capital gain otherwise. This
classification applies to a non-resident transferor in the same way as to a
resident.

Section 1.2 — Rate of tax, short-term gain
Short-term capital gain on the transfer of a security described in Section
1.1 is chargeable to tax at 20 per cent, with effect from transfers made on
or after 23 July 2024 (Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024). No deduction under
Chapter VI-A is available against such gain.

Section 1.3 — Rate of tax, long-term gain
Long-term capital gain on the transfer of a security described in Section
1.1 is chargeable to tax at 12.5 per cent, without indexation, with effect
from transfers made on or after 23 July 2024. Long-term capital gains of up
to Rs. 1,25,000 in a financial year, taken in aggregate across all such
transfers by the individual in that year, are exempt.

Section 1.4 — Deduction of tax at source
A person responsible for paying any sum chargeable to tax to a non-resident
must deduct tax at source at the rates in force at the time of payment or
credit, whichever is earlier. Unlike a resident seller transacting through a
recognised stock exchange, a non-resident's broker or depository
participant is not relieved of this obligation merely because securities
transaction tax has been paid on the transaction; tax is ordinarily withheld
on the sale proceeds credited to the non-resident's account, and a
certificate of the amount deducted must be issued to the non-resident.

Section 1.5 — Return of income
A non-resident individual to whom a capital gain described in this Section
has accrued must furnish a return of income for the relevant assessment
year, on Form ITR-2 (or Form ITR-3 where the individual also has income
from a business or profession), on or before the due date applicable to
that assessee under Section 139(1) — ordinarily 31 July following the close
of the financial year for an assessee not required to have accounts
audited, subject to any extension separately notified for that assessment
year. The return is what reconciles tax already withheld under Section 1.4
against the individual's actual liability computed under Sections 1.2 and
1.3, and is required whether the withheld amount fully discharges the
liability or not.
