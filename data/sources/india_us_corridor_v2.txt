FEMA Master Direction on Deposits & Accounts (NRI provisions) and the
Convention between the Government of the United States of America and the
Government of the Republic of India for the Avoidance of Double Taxation —
combined corridor guidance for a holder who is both an Indian citizen and a
United States tax resident

Instrument: RBI Master Direction on Deposits & Accounts (NRI provisions) +
            India-US Double Taxation Avoidance Convention +
            IRS Form 8802 / Form 6166 (US Certificate of Residency)
Issuing authority: Reserve Bank of India / Central Board of Direct Taxes /
                    Internal Revenue Service
Provision: Section 3.1 — FEMA reclassification of resident accounts

Section 3.1 — Application and FEMA reclassification of resident accounts
This corridor guidance applies only to a holder who is, at the same time, an
Indian citizen and a tax resident of the United States. Exactly as with the
India-Ireland corridor, the FEMA reclassification duty does not turn on
which country the holder became tax resident of — it turns on the fact of
becoming a non-resident Indian for tax purposes at all. An Indian citizen
who becomes resident outside India for tax purposes must not continue to
hold a resident (domestic) savings account in India; any such account must
be redesignated as a Non-Resident Ordinary (NRO) account, or a Non-Resident
External (NRE) account opened in its place, within a reasonable period of
the change in residency status. Continuing to operate a resident account
after ceasing to be an Indian tax resident is a contravention of FEMA
regulations administered by the Reserve Bank of India.

Section 3.2 — DTAA relief, Form 10F and the US Certificate of Residency
Where India-source income (such as interest on an NRO account, or dividends
or capital gains from Indian securities) arises to a holder who is tax
resident of the United States, relief from double taxation on that income
may be claimed under the India-US DTAA. Indian domestic law (Income-tax Act,
1961 ss. 90(4) and 90A(4)) conditions that relief on the holder furnishing a
Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) issued by the country of residence. The
United States does not issue a TRC in the Irish or Indian sense; the
equivalent document is IRS Form 6166 (Certificate of U.S. Tax Residency),
obtained by filing Form 8802 with the Internal Revenue Service. The holder
must obtain Form 6166 for the relevant year and furnish it, together with a
completed Form 10F, to the Indian payer or tax authority before the treaty
relief is applied.
