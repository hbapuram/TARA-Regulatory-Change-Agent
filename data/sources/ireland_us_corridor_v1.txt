IRS Form 1116 Instructions (Foreign Tax Credit) and the Convention between
the Government of Ireland and the Government of the United States of America
for the Avoidance of Double Taxation — combined corridor guidance for a
holder who is both an Irish citizen and a United States tax resident

Instrument: IRS Form 1116 Instructions (Foreign Tax Credit) +
            Ireland-US Double Taxation Avoidance Convention, Article 24
Issuing authority: Internal Revenue Service
Provision: Section 2.1 — Foreign tax credit for Irish capital gains tax paid

Section 2.1 — Application and the foreign tax credit
This corridor guidance applies only to a holder who is, at the same time, an
Irish citizen and a United States tax resident. Ireland charges capital
gains tax on the disposal of Irish-situated land and buildings irrespective
of the residence of the person disposing of it (see
domains/ireland-cgt-property.yaml, Section 1.1) — Ireland does not exempt a
US tax resident from this charge under the Ireland-US DTAA, because Article
24 of that Convention relieves double taxation by the credit method, not the
exemption method, for a US person's foreign-source income and gains. A
United States person who has paid Irish capital gains tax on a gain that is
also subject to US federal income tax on the same disposal may claim a
foreign tax credit for the Irish tax paid, computed and claimed on Form
1116, against their US tax liability on that gain, up to the Form 1116
limitation.

Section 2.2 — Substantiation
The foreign tax credit claimed on Form 1116 must be substantiated with
evidence of the foreign tax actually paid — in this case, the Irish Form CG1
return and the receipt for the capital gains tax payment made to the Office
of the Revenue Commissioners. This evidence must be retained and produced if
the credit is examined; a credit claimed without it may be disallowed.
