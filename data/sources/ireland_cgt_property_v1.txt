CAPITAL GAINS TAX — DISPOSAL OF LAND AND BUILDINGS SITUATED IN THE STATE
Office of the Revenue Commissioners
Consolidated extract for TARA domain pack `ireland-cgt-property`

Section 1.1 — Persons chargeable; specified assets
A person who is neither resident nor ordinarily resident in the State for a
year of assessment remains chargeable to capital gains tax for that year on
chargeable gains accruing on the disposal of specified assets, being (among
others) land in the State and buildings on such land. A gain on the disposal
of land or buildings situated in the State is therefore within the charge to
Irish capital gains tax irrespective of where the person disposing of it is
resident. A person who is ordinarily resident in the State but not resident
in it remains within the charge on the same assets.

Section 1.2 — Rate of charge and annual personal exemption
The standard rate of capital gains tax is 33 per cent of the chargeable gain.
The first EUR 1,270 of chargeable gains accruing to an individual in a year
of assessment is exempt from capital gains tax. The annual personal exemption
is not transferable between spouses or civil partners, but is available
separately to each.

Section 1.3 — Payment of tax
Capital gains tax is payable by reference to the period in which the disposal
is made. Where the disposal is made in the initial period, being 1 January to
30 November of a year of assessment, the tax is due and payable on or before
15 December in that same year of assessment. Where the disposal is made in
the later period, being 1 December to 31 December of a year of assessment,
the tax is due and payable on or before 31 January in the following year of
assessment.

Section 1.4 — Return of the disposal
A person who disposes of an asset must make a return of the disposal whether
or not any tax is payable, and whether or not relief is claimed. The return
is made on Form CG1, or within the income tax return (Form 11) where the
person is a chargeable person for income tax purposes, on or before 31
October of the year of assessment following the year in which the disposal
was made.

Section 1.5 — Deduction from consideration; clearance certificate
Where the consideration for the disposal of an asset exceeds EUR 500,000, or
EUR 1,000,000 in the case of a house or apartment, the person acquiring the
asset is obliged to deduct 15 per cent of the consideration and remit it to
the Revenue Commissioners, unless the person disposing of the asset produces
a capital gains tax clearance certificate (Form CG50A) obtained in advance of
completion. An amount so deducted is available as a credit against the
capital gains tax liability of the person disposing of the asset.

Section 1.6 — Principal private residence relief
Relief from capital gains tax is available on the disposal of a dwelling
house which has been occupied by the individual as their only or main
residence throughout their period of ownership, together with land occupied
as its gardens or grounds up to one acre exclusive of the site of the
dwelling house. Where the dwelling house has been so occupied for part only
of the period of ownership, the relief is restricted by reference to the
proportion of that period for which it was so occupied. The last 12 months of
the period of ownership are treated as a period of occupation in every case
where the dwelling house was the individual's only or main residence at some
time. A computation showing the apportionment relied on must be retained to
substantiate a partial claim.
