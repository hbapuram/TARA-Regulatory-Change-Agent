# TARA — Seven-Minute LLM-First Pitch Script

**Target duration:** 6:30, leaving a 30-second buffer inside the seven-minute limit.

**Delivery rule:** lead with the human consequence, show one working AI path, then explain the controls. Do not introduce internal agent names or IDs in the core pitch.

---

## Slide 1 — TARA: From regulatory change to reviewed action (0:00–0:30)

> “A rule can change in one sentence, but the work it creates does not. Someone must work out who is affected, what they need to do, and whether the proof is right. TARA turns that change into a clear, reviewable action. Its LLM investigates through guarded MCP tools; deterministic controls decide the consequential checks.”

Transition: “Here is the failure that makes that distinction matter.”

---

## Slide 2 — One small rule change can make a plausible calculation wrong (0:30–1:20)

> “Meet Maeve. Revenue changes the relevant rate from 41% to 38% for an event in 2026. A 41% calculation can look perfectly coherent, but it is now based on an older rule. For a cross-border advisory team, the challenge is not publishing the new rule. It is connecting that change to the right person, the right action, and the right evidence—without asking a professional to start every review from scratch.”

Transition: “TARA connects those steps in one controlled loop.”

---

## Slide 3 — TARA turns one change into a safe, reviewable next step (1:20–2:05)

> “The model first discovers the permitted domain, source, and case tools. It then uses those tools to investigate the request. TARA’s deterministic controls compare the source versions, apply dates and thresholds, create the action plan, and check the evidence. The point is not to let a model improvise compliance. The point is to let it navigate a governed workflow intelligently, while the important calculations remain exact and inspectable.”

Transition: “Let’s run that path live.”

---

## Slide 4 — The working AI agent: source to action to evidence (2:05–3:35)

> “I will run the Maeve case. First, the top of the page confirms that the AI/MCP path is ready. The visible orchestration trace shows the LLM discovering the available source and case tools. That is important: it is not guessing an identifier or relying on a hidden prompt.
>
> Now, TARA shows what changed: 41% became 38%. It applies the 2026 event date, which selects the current rule and creates four next steps. Finally, I test the evidence. I apply the old 41% rate. TARA returns it with a plain-language reason. I restore the 38% example; TARA accepts it and updates the proof record.
>
> We have shown an AI agent doing real investigation, but we have not asked it to calculate the rate or decide a deadline.”

**Presenter fallback:** if the AI/MCP route is unavailable, say: “The page has switched to its labelled deterministic backup. It runs the same controlled source, date, and evidence checks; the captured replay is available if the hosted service is unavailable too.”

Transition: “The boundary between these two layers is the product’s trust model.”

---

## Slide 5 — The LLM investigates; deterministic controls decide (3:35–4:35)

> “The LLM’s job is to understand the request, discover the guarded tools, sequence the investigation, and explain the verified result. It never owns the source rate, event-date selection, threshold comparison, arithmetic, or evidence outcome. Those decisions are deterministic Python controls. Each result has a readable MCP trace and a tamper-evident proof record. When a required fact is missing, TARA fails closed: it asks for review instead of inventing a conclusion.”

Transition: “That architecture lets us begin with a focused customer.”

---

## Slide 6 — Start with the team that carries the cost of missed change (4:35–5:35)

> “Our beachhead is a cross-border tax advisory practice. That is where a missed rule change creates professional risk, unbillable research, and client friction. Our pricing hypothesis is a monitored rule-pack and usage tier, not a generic chatbot subscription. The differentiator is that TARA does not stop at summarising a regulation. It connects the change to a case, an exact action, an evidence check, and a proof record. These are hypotheses we need to validate with professionals and a design partner—not traction we are claiming today.”

Transition: “The code is ready for the next kind of proof.”

---

## Slide 7 — A working prototype now; the right validation next (5:35–6:30)

> “Today, TARA has 115 passing automated tests, 12 guarded MCP tools, nine rule packs, eight executable acceptance checks, a live LLM/MCP path, and two clear fallbacks. We are candid about what remains: independent domain review, buyer interviews, one controlled pilot, and governed source ingestion.
>
> Our ask is specific. We are looking for an Irish tax or domain reviewer and one cross-border advisory practice willing to help design a safe pilot. If we can prove that this shortens review time and reduces false-positive work without compromising control, TARA has a credible path from prototype to product. Thank you.”
